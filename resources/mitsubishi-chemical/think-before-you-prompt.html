Think Before You Prompt — Question · Map · Process

How to approach any job with AI: interrogate it with five questions, map it into labelled steps, then let AI process its steps one prompt at a time — with you checking in between. Worked example: the end-of-shift handover.

Topic 1

The prompt lottery

You type "write my end of shift handover report". Copilot writes a polite, generic, 300-word report about a shift that never happened. You conclude AI is useless for real work. Most people stop here.

The problem isn't the tool and it isn't even the prompt. It's the approach: you asked for the whole job in one line, with no material, no decisions made, and no way to check the result. GCES (Copilot 101 and Prompt Coach, cards 11 and 12 on the hub) fixes how you write a prompt. This page fixes how you decide what to prompt for.

The one idea: think before you prompt. Interrogate the job, map it into steps, decide which steps are AI's and which are yours, then let AI process its steps one at a time.
Topic 2

The system — Question · Map · Process

1

QUESTION

Interrogate the job before you open Copilot. Five questions, always the same five:

  • Outcome — what exactly must exist when I'm done?
  • Audience — who uses it, and what do they do next?
  • Good looks like — length, format, language, tone?
  • Inputs — what do I already have (notes, log, photo, last week's version)?
  • Stays with me — what must I decide, check or sign myself?
2

MAP

Write the job as a list of steps, in order. Label every step:

  • AI-do AI does it, I check it — reformat, summarise, translate, draft, group, list.
  • AI-help AI proposes, I decide — options, wording, "what did I miss".
  • Me-only judgement, safety, sign-off, anything with personal or confidential data.

Note what each step takes in and hands out — the output of step 2 is the input of step 3.

3

PROCESS

Run the map. One step, one prompt, one check:

  • Write each AI step as a GCES prompt; paste the real input as the Source.
  • Verify the output against the source before feeding it to the next step (S.U.R.E.).
  • Do the Me-only steps yourself, in the chain, where they sit.
  • When it works, save the chain as a recipe in PARA → Resources. Next shift, reuse it.
Why the labels matter. The label is where the thinking happens. Deciding that "which items are URGENT" is Me-only is the decision that keeps you in charge of the shift — and it is exactly the decision the one-line prompt skipped.

Worked example — end-of-shift handover

A shift supervisor's real job, done the Q·M·P way. Download the sample notes and run it yourself.

3 · PROCESS — the prompts, in order

Step 1 · AI-help

Goal: Turn my raw shift notes into a clean event table. Context: I am the shift supervisor on Line 3 (compounding, glass-filled PA66). The notes below are in the order things happened, typed on my phone, mixed English/Malay shorthand. Expectations: One table with columns Time | Machine | What happened | Action taken | Status (Open/Closed). Keep my wording where it is unclear and put "?" rather than guessing. Do not add events I did not write. Source: [paste the sample notes here]

Verify: count the rows against your notes. Thirteen lines in, thirteen rows out. Anything marked "?" — fix it now, before step 2.

Step 2 · AI-do

Using the table you just made: group the rows by Machine. Add a column "Flag" and mark YES for any row where Status is Open, or where the event contains the words trip, alarm, reject, stop, leak or contamination. Show the grouped table only — no commentary.

Step 3 · Me-only

Read the flagged rows. Mark two as URGENT. In the sample notes the obvious two are the taped hose on M3 (still taped at 06:40) and the M2 amp problem on lot 4471 — Copilot doesn't know the dryer alarm on D2 has come up three shifts running. You do.

Step 4 · AI-do

Goal: Write my end-of-shift handover from the grouped table. Context: Reader is the incoming shift supervisor and his technicians, reading on phones at 7am. I have marked two rows URGENT. Expectations: Maximum 6 bullet points, plain English, no more than 15 words per bullet. The two URGENT items come first and start with the word URGENT. End with a short list titled "Actions for next shift" (max 5 items, each starting with a verb). No greetings, no sign-off. Source: The grouped table above, plus my URGENT marks: M3 hopper loader hose (taped, needs proper hose) and M2 high screw amp on lot 4471 (suspect moisture / dryer D2).

Step 5 · AI-help

Under the "Actions for next shift" list, add the same list in simple Bahasa Melayu and in simple Tamil. Keep machine names and numbers exactly as in the English. Keep each line short. Do not translate the rest of the handover.
Verify step 5 with a person. That is what AI-help means: AI proposes, a human who reads the language checks. Then step 6 — you read the whole thing once more and send it.
Save the recipe. Five prompts, two decisions. Put them in a note called Recipe — shift handover in PARA → Resources. Tomorrow it is a three-minute job. The MCMS role prompt library (card 10 on the hub) gives you the same recipe pattern for your own job function.
Topic 4

The 8 best practices

  1. Ask before you prompt. Five questions, every time. If you can't answer "outcome", you're not ready to type.
  2. One step, one prompt. Never ask for the whole job in one line.
  3. Give the input, not the job title. Paste the notes, the log, the photo — not "write a report".
  4. Label every step AI-do / AI-help / Me-only. Safety decisions, sign-offs and disposition are always Me-only.
  5. Verify before you chain. Check each output against the source before it becomes the next input. S.U.R.E. applies to Copilot too.
  6. Never paste what you can't email outside. NRIC/FIN, customer names and prices, process conditions, plant drawings and specifications marked confidential, photos of screens showing any of these. Card 8 (Traffic-Light Data) sorts what is Green, Amber and Red.
  7. Fix the step, not the whole thing. Wrong output? Change that one prompt. Don't start over.
  8. Save the recipe. The chain that worked goes into PARA → Resources with a name. Next shift it's a 3-minute job.

Topic 6

Knowledge check

✔ Knowledge check

In the handover example, why is 'decide which items are URGENT' labelled Me-only?

It is a judgement call that depends on what the supervisor knows about the last three shifts. AI can flag; the supervisor decides.

✔ Knowledge check

What is the difference between AI-do and AI-help?

AI-do — reformatting, summarising, grouping: AI does it, you check. AI-help — options, wording, translation: AI proposes, you decide.

✔ Knowledge check

What should happen between step 1 and step 2 of any Q·M·P chain?

Verify before you chain. A wrong row in step 1 becomes a wrong flag in step 2 and a wrong URGENT in step 4.
