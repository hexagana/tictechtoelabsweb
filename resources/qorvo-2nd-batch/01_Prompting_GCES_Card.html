The one method we all use

The GCES card

One shared method, so we can compare, critique and improve each other's prompts in the same words after the course.

What you want. Start with a verb.

Draft · Summarise · Compare · Explain · Turn this into...

What the tool needs to know: who you are, who will read it, the situation, what matters.

I am a production planner. The readers are the operations managers and...

Format, length, tone and audience.

5 bullets · a table with these columns · under 150 words · plain English

What to base the answer on, and what to do when something is missing.

Use only the attached log. If it is not in the log, say so instead of guessing.

Role, context, task, format, constraints. These five elements of an effective prompt all fit on the card: the task is your Goal, the role and the situation are Context, and the format and length are Expectations. GCES adds S, Source, the constraint that matters most: what to base the answer on. What you leave out, Copilot fills in.
The four-line test: before you press Enter, point at one line for each letter. If a letter has no line, that is where the answer will go wrong.
Hands-on · 15 minutes

Before and after

Run the one-line prompt, then the GCES version, in the same Copilot. Put the two answers side by side.

Planning: the weekly build plan misses

Before
Summarise the build plan misses this week.
After, with GCES
Goal: Summarise this week's build plan misses for the Monday operations meeting. Context: I am a senior production planner at a semiconductor assembly and test site. The readers are the operations managers and the planning team. They want to know which devices missed their commit, by how many units, why, and when we recover. Expectations: Maximum 6 bullet points, biggest shortfall first, with the units short on each. Then a table with four columns: Device | Units short | Reason | Recovery week. Plain English, no greeting or sign-off. Source: Use only the commit-vs-actual table pasted below. If a reason or a recovery week is not in the table, write "not recorded" instead of guessing. [paste the commit-vs-actual table here]

Office: an announcement email

Before
Write an email about the new leave process.
After, with GCES
Goal: Draft an email announcing that annual leave will be applied for in the HR portal from 1 December, instead of on the paper form. Context: I work in HR. The readers are all staff, including production operators who read email on their phones. Approvals stay with the same supervisor as today. Expectations: Under 150 words, friendly and plain. Include a subject line, three numbered steps, and one closing line saying who to ask. Source: Use only the facts in this prompt. Do not add dates, rules or links that I have not given you.

Compare the two answers

  1. Which one could you send or use without rewriting it?
  2. Find one thing the one-line version made up. Which GCES line stopped that in the second version?
  3. Which letter made the biggest difference? Most groups say Source or Expectations.
Structured iteration

Fix one part at a time

A disappointing answer usually means one letter is weak. Find it, change only that line, and run it again.

If the answer is...Change this partFor example
About the wrong thing, or answers a different questionGGoalSay exactly what you want: "a 6-bullet summary", not "something about the build plan"
Generic, and could be about any companyCContextAdd who it is for and the situation they are in
Right content, wrong shape: too long, wrong tone, no tableEExpectations"Maximum 4 bullets", "as a table with these columns", "for a non-technical reader"
Full of facts, names or figures you did not give itSSource"Use only the attached file", "if it is missing, say so"

Change one part per try

Then you know what made the difference, and you can explain what you changed.

Reply in the same chat

For small fixes, just reply: "Shorter, maximum 4 bullets." Start a new chat only when you change the Goal completely.

Stop after three tries

If it is still not usable, the input may be missing, or the task may be a poor candidate for AI (see Opportunity Recognition). Doing it yourself is a valid answer.

Add-ons

Personas, examples and formats

Lines you can add to any GCES prompt to lock in the style you want for reports, logs and specifications. These are prompt modifiers: tone, length, audience and style.

Add-onA line you can pasteWhat it does
Persona"Act as an experienced production planner writing for the operations director."Sets the tone and vocabulary. It does not give the tool knowledge it does not have.
Example"Here is last month's summary. Match its layout, headings and tone."The fastest way to get your house style. Remove confidential details from the example first.
Table"As a table with columns: Item | Owner | Due date | Status."Output you can paste straight into Excel or Word.
Length"Maximum 120 words" or "one line per item".Stops long, padded answers.
Gaps"List any questions you need answered before this is final."Makes missing context visible instead of invented.

Grounding in your own files

An answer grounded in your own document beats one from the open web. How you give Copilot the file depends on which Copilot your account has.
