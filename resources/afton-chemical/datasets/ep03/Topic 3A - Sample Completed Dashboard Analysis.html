Intelligent Insights

🎯 Volume — not pricing — is the revenue lever

March had the highest $/pax ($375) of any month, yet delivered the lowest revenue ($5,250). Volatility is almost entirely a scheduling / pipeline-fill problem. Each additional class at typical fill rates adds ~$4,200–$4,900 to the month.

✅ Funded-class pricing has been quietly fixed

Funded classes used to underprice severely (Jan: $162/pax, Feb: $160/pax). March (NSRC) and April (HL Material) both hit $350/pax exactly. If this discipline holds, the revenue floor is structurally lifted by ~$3K–$4K per funded class.

⚠ Trainer concentration is the #1 operational risk

Khai delivers 74% of revenue on average, 100% in March. Zayed's utilisation is wildly inconsistent (30%, 19%, 0%, 31%). This is a single-person-of-failure problem the monthly dashboards keep flagging without a structural fix.

📊 No clear seasonal pattern yet — sample is too small

4 months isn't enough to call seasonality. The March dip looks more like a sales-pipeline gap than a calendar effect (CNY was Feb 17, well clear of March). Treat March as an open question, not a predictable trough, until another year of data is in.

📈 Pricing trend is up; volume trend is flat

$/pax went $258 → $303 → $375 → $350. Class count went 3 → 4 → 2 → 3 (no trend). The team is getting better at extracting value per booking but not at generating bookings. Sales pipeline likely needs more attention than pricing.

💪 Margins are exceptional

Reported expenses are tiny ($105–$359/month). Gross margin runs ~98%+. The economic model is fundamentally healthy — the problem is throughput, not profitability per class.

Assumptions & Limitations

What this analysis is built on

  • Source data: the four monthly HTML dashboards (Jan–Apr 2026). Not cross-validated against the underlying TRAINER PAYMENT TRACKER ...xlsx backup files — recommend reconciliation before using these numbers in any client-facing or investor-facing context.
  • Sample size: 4 months is small. Confidence intervals on the projection are wide — treat scenarios as ranges, not point estimates.
  • No pipeline data: projection assumes historical class-generation rate continues. Actual May–Aug bookings (if known) would tighten the forecast significantly.
  • Allan commission methodology: inconsistent across the 4 months, so historical "net revenue after commission" comparisons are not directly comparable.
  • Currency: SGD throughout.
  • Seasonality: not modelled — would require at least 12 months of data to identify.
  • Trainer capacity: projections assume current trainer roster. A second active trainer would expand the bull case ceiling.
